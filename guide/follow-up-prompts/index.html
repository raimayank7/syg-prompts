Follow-up prompts: improving an answer step by step

People often judge an AI assistant by its first answer and give up if that answer is poor. The better way is to treat the first answer as a draft and steer it. A conversation of three or four short messages usually produces something far better than any single prompt, however carefully written.

Why follow-ups work

Within one conversation the assistant keeps your earlier messages and its own answers in view. So you do not need to repeat the facts. You only say what to change. This is quick, and it is also how you would work with a human assistant: "Good, but shorter, and drop the second point."

One change at a time

If you ask for five changes in one message, some may be missed or may pull against each other. Ask for the biggest change first, look at the result, and then ask for the next. Structure before wording, wording before polish.

When to start a new conversation

Follow-ups are not always the answer. Start afresh when:

  • The conversation has become long. In very long conversations an assistant can lose track of early instructions.
  • It keeps returning to a mistake. Once a wrong idea is in the conversation, it tends to come back. A clean start with a better first prompt is faster.
  • You are changing topic. Leftover context from the old task can colour the new one.
  • You now know what you should have said. Take what you learnt, write a fuller first prompt and begin again.

When you restart, paste in the facts and the best version so far, so that you do not lose your progress.

Do not argue with it

If an assistant insists on something you know to be false, repeating yourself rarely helps, and it may eventually agree only to be agreeable, which proves nothing. State the correct fact once, as a fact: "The date is 14 March. Use that." If it matters, check the fact from a proper source yourself.

A simple routine

  1. Send a clear first prompt.
  2. Read the whole answer.
  3. Fix the biggest problem with one short follow-up.
  4. Repeat once or twice.
  5. Check the facts yourself, then make the last edits by hand.

The final step matters. The result should sound like you, and the last ten per cent is usually quicker to do yourself than to describe.
